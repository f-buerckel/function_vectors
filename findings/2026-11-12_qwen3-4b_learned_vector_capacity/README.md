# Capacity of learned vectors on Qwen3-4B (2026-11-12)

The [learned vectors](../2026-11-12_qwen3-4b_learned_vector/README.md) held 4 bound facts per person. This tests how held-out accuracy changes with the number of facts per vector (2, 4 and 8), which matters for comparing with PRAG's per-document LoRA adapters.

## Reproduce

- **Code:** commit `b3eed6b0b201890a210c0d009e46b4c42ffee2f7` on branch `qwen-fv-extraction`
- **Data:** `dataset_files/knowledge/fictional_people_capacity.json`, generated by `make_capacity_dataset.py` (seed 0). 8 fictional people with 8 city facts each, drawn without repetition from 16 single-token cities. Setup-only.
- **Command**, from `src/`, for k = 2, 4, 8:

  ```bash
  python learn_knowledge_vector.py --data_path ../dataset_files/knowledge/fictional_people_capacity.json \
      --n_facts $k --layers 6 12 --save_path ../results/knowledge_capacity_learned/facts_$k
  ```

  Defaults: positions `last` and `name`, 150 Adam steps with learning rate 0.1, loss scale 1024, `--seed 42`.
- **Environment:** AMD RX 9070 XT, ROCm 7.2.1 under WSL2, torch 2.9.1, transformers 4.57.6, float16.
- **Runtime:** about 27 minutes for all three (8–10 minutes each, 32 vectors each).

## Setup

- **Fact types:** born, work, study and home city as before, plus married in, grew up in, holidays in and plans to retire in. Each has a demonstration question, 2 extraction paraphrases (all used for training) and 1 held-out paraphrase.
- **Facts per vector:** each person's first k facts in the order above. The born and work city appear in every k, so their accuracy can be compared across k.
- **Closed set:** all 16 cities for every k, so chance is 1/16 throughout.
- **Bag-of-answers baseline:** a vector that gives one of its k cities regardless of the question gets 1/k.
- Everything else is as in the learned-vector experiment.

## Results

**Training questions always fit; held-out accuracy falls with more facts.**

| Position | Layer | 2 facts | 4 facts | 8 facts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| last | 6 | 0.88 | 0.84 | 0.69 |
| last | 12 | 0.81 | 0.84 | 0.77 |
| name | 6 | 0.94 | 0.81 | 0.58 |
| name | 12 | 0.94 | 0.72 | 0.66 |
| Bag-of-answers baseline | | 0.50 | 0.25 | 0.125 |
| No vector | | 0.06 | 0.03 | 0.03 |

- Training questions are 100% right for every k, position and layer (largest final loss 0.006, at 8 facts).
- At 8 facts, held-out accuracy is still 4.6–6.2 times the bag-of-answers baseline.
- Name vectors are best at 2 facts but degrade fastest; last-token vectors hold up best at 8 facts.
- Share of people with all held-out questions right: 62–88% at 2 facts, 38% at 4, 0% at 8.

**The same facts get worse as more are added.** Held-out accuracy by fact type, averaged over positions and layers:

| Fact type | 2 facts | 4 facts | 8 facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| born city | 0.81 | 0.56 | 0.44 |
| work city | 0.97 | 0.78 | 0.62 |
| study city | | 1.00 | 0.94 |
| home city | | 0.88 | 0.69 |
| married city | | | 0.69 |
| grew-up city | | | 0.62 |
| holiday city | | | 0.94 |
| retirement city | | | 0.44 |

**Errors are confusions between the vector's own facts.** Of 116 wrong held-out answers over all k, 115 are another city stored in the same vector, and 1 is a city not belonging to the person.

**Side effects on unrelated questions** (capitals still right): 56–88% for last-token vectors and 66–100% for name vectors, with no clear trend in k.

## Interpretation

- **One vector can fit at least 8 bound facts on the training questions,** at the size of one hidden state (2,560 numbers, 5 kB in float16). What limits it is generalisation to new phrasings, not fitting.
- **Adding facts causes interference:** the same born- and work-city questions get worse, and the errors are confusions with the other stored facts. The vector keeps the cities but loses some of the mapping from question to city on unseen phrasings.
- **For the PRAG comparison:** a single vector is tiny compared with a LoRA adapter, but here it holds only a handful of facts reliably. A real document has many more facts, so it would need several vectors, more layers or positions, or more training phrasings.
- **Limits:**
  - Only 3 training phrasings per fact and 1 held-out phrasing; more phrasings would likely improve generalisation.
  - Fixed training (150 steps, no regularisation).
  - 8 people, cities only.

## What we investigate next

1. **More training phrasings per fact,** to see whether the generalisation gap closes as the number of facts grows.
2. **More than one vector per document** (several layers or positions), to raise capacity.
3. **Amortisation:** a network that maps a document to its vector, so documents don't each need optimisation.
